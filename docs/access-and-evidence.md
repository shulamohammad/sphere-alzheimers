# Access and evidence notes

Reviewed 2026-11-08. These are documentation observations, not findings from downloaded data.

Primary sources:
- Portal: https://adrc-sphere.stanford.edu/
- Method paper linked by portal: https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.01.748580
- Repository linked by portal: https://purl.stanford.edu/sm297vv5829

The portal describes a synthetic cohort derived from 644 participants, combining clinical and biological measurements. It names MoCA, CDR, targeted blood biomarkers, PET-derived features, and other modalities. Actual exports, column names, temporal structure and sample overlap remain unverified.

The download dialog currently requires an account/API key or a signed-in SPHERE session, plus agreement acceptance. No credentials have been entered and no agreement has been accepted by the assistant.

Observed agreement provisions requiring the user's review include project-specific use, restrictions on redistribution and unauthorized access, data-security and recordkeeping duties, a prohibition on re-identification/reconstruction, acknowledgment requirements, and Stanford review periods before public disclosure of results. The full agreement, rather than this summary, governs access. Do not upload raw data to external services without establishing that use is permitted. Keep credentials outside the project.

## Suggested registration project description

Retrospective methods development using SPHERE synthetic Alzheimer’s Disease Research Center data to evaluate diagnostic-group classification under missing measurements and constrained measurement budgets. The study will compare simple machine-learning baselines, assess calibration and uncertainty, and examine subgroup performance where sample sizes permit. Initial analyses will use demographic/diagnosis variables, targeted blood biomarkers, and selected imaging measurements as needed. The study will not attempt re-identification or reconstruction of source records, and will not deploy clinical or insurance eligibility decisions. Results will be reported as synthetic-data methods findings with appropriate limitations.
